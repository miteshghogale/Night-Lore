Late on a cold November night in 1966, two young couples were driving past an abandoned World War II munitions facility near Point Pleasant, West Virginia. The locals called it the TNT area, a overgrown property filled with concrete storage bunkers.

As their car headlights swept past an old generator building, the driver slammed on the brakes. Standing near the door was a grey figure over seven feet tall with a ten-foot wingspan folded against its back. Where its head should have been, two massive red eyes glowed in the dark like hot embers. Panicked, the driver shifted into gear and sped down Highway 62 at over one hundred miles per hour. The creature rose into the air and glided directly above the car roof without flapping its wings, keeping pace with them all the way to the town line.

Over the next thirteen months, more than one hundred citizens filed formal statements with Mason County Sheriff George Johnson. The witnesses included police officers, commercial pilots, and local farmers. Many reported strange physical symptoms after seeing the figure, including severe eye irritation and home electronics malfunctioning. Others spoke of strange men in dark suits coming to their doors and warning them to stay silent.

Everything came to a sudden, tragic head on December 15, 1967. Right at five o'clock during rush-hour traffic, the Silver Bridge connecting Point Pleasant to Kanauga, Ohio, suffered a sudden structural failure. Forty-six people lost their lives when the suspension bridge collapsed into the icy Ohio River. Right after the disaster, the sightings of the winged figure stopped completely.

## What Actually Happened Here

The Mothman reports present a rare case where eyewitness sightings became intertwined with one of the worst civil engineering disasters in American history.

Researchers who view the creature as a genuine anomaly point to the consistency of the police reports. More than one hundred people who did not know each other described the exact same height, color, wingspan, and glowing red eyes, and many reported identical radio and television interference during the exact same weeks.

Skeptical wildlife biologists offer a straightforward explanation. Researchers from West Virginia University suggested that witnesses were seeing Sandhill Cranes. These large migratory birds stand over five feet tall, have seven-foot wingspans, and feature bright red skin patches around their eyes. If a small flock of cranes went off course during winter migration and settled in the overgrown TNT reserve, startled drivers seeing them in high-beam headlights could easily spark a period of mass panic that became forever tied to the bridge collapse.
