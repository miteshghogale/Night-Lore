## The 1949 Jesuit Diary

In 1971, William Peter Blatty published *The Exorcist*, drawing inspiration from news reports of a 14-year-old boy under the pseudonym "Roland Doe" (later revealed as Ronald Hunkeler). The events occurred in Cottage City, Maryland, and St. Louis, Missouri, in 1949.

Father Raymond J. Bishop maintained a 26-page handwritten diary detailing 30 consecutive exorcism sessions authorized by the Archbishop of Washington.

```
JESUIT DIARY DOCUMENTATION // ST. LOUIS 1949
LOCATION: Alexian Brothers Hospital Psychiatric Wing
SUBJECT: Ronald Hunkeler (Age 14)
OFFICIATING CLERGY: Fr. William S. Bowdern, S.J. & Fr. Raymond Bishop, S.J.
```

## Documented Phenomena vs Hollywood Mythos

The original 1949 diary entries record physical occurrences that differ substantially from the movie:
* **Recorded**: Scratches forming blood-wounds spelling out words like "HELL" and "EVIL" across the boy's chest; bed vibrations moving across the room floor; objects sliding off nightstands.
* **Hollywood Fiction**: Head spinning 360 degrees, levitation to the ceiling, and green vomit were cinematic inventions added by Blatty.

## Paranormal vs. Skeptical Hypothesis

| Hypothesis | Arguments & Evidence |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Demonic Possession** | Sworn daily diary entries by educated Jesuit priests noting Latin responses uttered by a child with no prior Latin training. |
| **Psychiatric / Psychological** | Journalist Mark Opsasnick's 1999 investigation concluded Roland was a emotionally disturbed child who used scratching tricks to avoid attending school, amplified by hyper-religious family expectations. |

With conflicting testimonies between Jesuit diaries and subsequent investigative journalism, the true medical and spiritual nature of the 1949 case remains **ongoing**.
